## 2. The hard truth first

**Tags and titles alone will not get you to $1,000.** Here is the arithmetic.

Your average listing price across the catalog is **$9.35** (computed from the 37 listing prices captured off your shop grid). At 11 sales in 4 months you are running ~2.75 sales/month. $1,000 in Q4 at $9.35 AOV = **107 sales = ~36/month = 13x your current rate.** No amount of keyword work multiplies traffic 14x in 10 weeks.

Three structural problems are capping you, and two of them are more urgent than tags:

**Problem A — the 1-star AI review is poisoning your entire crochet line.** It is not just about the axolotl. A buyer reads "this product is very misleading, use a REAL photo" and applies it to all 17 crochet patterns. Your crochet section has 0 five-star reviews and 1 one-star. That section is currently a liability, not an asset.

**Problem B — your About section describes products you do not sell.** It promises "fleece-lined sublimation socks and vibrant Direct-to-Film (DTF) apparel to natural rubber yoga mats" and "stainless steel for our mugs." All 38 of your listings are digital downloads. A buyer who reads that and finds zero physical goods concludes the shop description is boilerplate — which then makes them question the spreadsheet quality too.

**Problem C — a flat 25%-off sale on 100% of inventory is not a sale.** Your competitors discount **30%, 40%, 50%, 60%, 67%** and rotate it. A permanent uniform discount reads as "this is just the price," kills urgency, and caps your margin. See the evidence in §4.

## 4. The five title patterns that are actually ranking

Reading across all four search results, every winner follows the same structure:

```
[Specific product name] | [2-3 concrete sub-features] | [Platform] [Format]
```

Real examples that prove it:
- `Pet Sitting Business Tracker Bundle | HTML App, Excel & GS sheets, Printable PDFs | Clients, Bookings, Invoices, Expenses, Mileage Logs`
- `Ultimate Budget by Paycheck Spreadsheet Google Sheets & Excel | Paycheck Budgeting Planner, Bills, Savings, Debt Tracker`
- `Christmas Snow - Reversible Tree Skirt Crochet pattern - 5 feet wide - Standard US terms`

**Your titles are doing the opposite.** Compare:

| Yours | Problem |
|---|---|
| `Personal Finance Bundle: Budget, Debt, Net Worth Trackers (Excel/Google Sheets)` | 77 chars — you're wasting 63 of your 140 characters. Colons and parentheses don't read as keywords. |
| `Property Investment Analyzer Pro, Cap Rate, Cash Flow, DSCR Calculator (Excel Template)` | "Pro" is filler. Missing the actual search terms: *rental property analysis, real estate investment, BRRRR, rental calculator*. |
| `Secret Santa & White Elephant Party Tracker, Excel Sheets, Budget Rules (Digital Download)` | "Digital Download" is dead weight — Etsy already badges it. "Budget Rules" is not a search term. |

Rules extracted from the winners:

1. **Use `|` or `-` separators, not `,` `:` `(` `)`.** Every top result does.
2. **Never spend characters on `(Digital Download)` or `(PDF Download)`.** Etsy shows that badge automatically. That's 18-20 wasted characters per title.
3. **Name the audience, not the adjective.** "for Contractors", "for Pet Sitters", "ADHD-friendly" — these convert because the buyer sees themselves.
4. **Front-load the exact search phrase in the first 40 characters.** Etsy weights the beginning of the title heaviest.
5. **Put a number or spec in if it's true.** "5 feet wide", "52-Tab", "3 Sizes", "11 Spreadsheets". Your 52-Tab wedding planner title does this well — copy that move.

## 6. Pricing: kill the flat 25%

Every competitor I read uses a *deep, varied* discount: 30%, 35%, 40%, 42%, 45%, 50%, 60%, 67%. You use 25% on all 38 items.

**Action:**
1. Raise the **list price** on the seven niche business tools so the sale price lands where it is now or higher. Never lower a sale price — raise the anchor.
2. **Vary the discount**: 40% on the business tools, 45% on seasonal, 30% on the premium bundle. Uniformity reads as fake.
3. **Take 3-5 items fully off sale** — specifically Construction Estimate and School Management. Full-price items make the discounted ones look real.
